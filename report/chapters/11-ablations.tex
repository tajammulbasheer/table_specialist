\section{Repairer strength}

The only variable in the repair pilot (\cref{tab:gen-repair-pilot}) is the repairer: the rejected pairs, diagnoses,
retries and validator are the same. Base recovered 7 of 237 pairs; Instruct recovered 23. Repairer quality therefore
determines how much data repair recovers, and, as \cref{sec:ext-control} suggests, possibly what that data is worth.

\section{Hyper-parameter sensitivity}

\paragraph{DPO} (\cref{tab:gen-dpo-dev}). Five settings were compared on dev. The learning rate dominates: at
$2\times10^{-5}$, raising $\beta$ from 0.1 to 0.5 changes nothing ($-0.0208$ and $-0.0244$ against bestof4). Lowering
the rate to $5\times10^{-6}$ or $10^{-6}$ removes most of the damage. No setting improves on bestof4.

\paragraph{Self-consistency temperature} (\cref{tab:gen-sc-dev}). The vote's dev gain rises with temperature: $+0.0218$
at 0.5, $+0.0260$ at 0.7 and $+0.0295$ at 1.0. Higher temperatures give more diverse samples to vote among.

\begin{observation}
Vanilla models gain 0.11--0.15 from the few-shot prompt. Every fine-tuned model scores slightly \emph{lower} with it

\section{Scoring sensitivity}

\input{generated/scoring_sensitivity.tex}

\begin{observation}
\begin{itemize}
\item On NL-to-SQL, the corrected score moves the zero-shot mean of 4 by at most $+0.0071$. Almost all of the change
is on Spider (up to $+0.0284$, base vanilla), where models add or omit \texttt{ORDER BY} and the repository compares
row order.
\item On NL-to-Scala, the correction is larger, $+0.021$ to $+0.025$ on the mean, and up to $+0.099$ on Spider.
Spark returns rows in no fixed order, and the repository's Scala comparison is order-sensitive.
\end{itemize}
No conclusion of this report changes sign under the corrected score.
\end{observation}

\section{Seed sensitivity}

\begin{observation}
Seed-to-seed standard deviations of \Mviii{} are 0.003--0.023 (\cref{tab:gen-round-stats}).
\begin{itemize}
\item Base TS is the most seed-sensitive at round~3 (SD 0.0210, range 0.5244--0.5660).
\item Base repair-TS is the least (SD 0.0051, range 0.5801--0.5901), as is Instruct repair-TS at round~2 (SD 0.0026).
\item Single-seed differences between methods are often of the size of the seed spread. For example, bestof4 against
TS round~1 is $+0.0065$, $+0.0282$ and $-0.0156$ by seed.
\end{itemize}
This is why every comparison reports per-seed differences, and why only consistent signs across all three seeds are
called consistent.
\end{observation}

\begin{interpretation}
Three seeds are enough to detect the large effects in this report: TS against NV at round~3, repair-TS on base, and
DPO. They are not enough to rank methods whose differences are below about 0.01, such as Instruct repair-TS against
Instruct TS, or bestof4 against TS. The paired case bootstrap gives narrow intervals ($\pm0.005$) but measures only
test-set sampling; it says nothing about how a different training seed would behave.
\end{interpretation}

\section{Round sensitivity}

The paper does not say which round it reports, so the choice of round is itself a sensitivity.
\begin{itemize}
\item Base: every arm except NV is best at round~3.
\item Instruct TS: best at round~2 on 2 of 3 seeds. The mean of the best round per seed is 0.6245 \ZSiv{}, against
0.6113 for round~3.
\item Instruct repair-TS: best at round~2 on the mean (\ZSiv{} 0.6241).
\item NL-to-Scala repair-TS: flat from round~2 to round~3.
\end{itemize}
Reporting the last round, as we do in the headline tables, is the conservative choice for Instruct.
